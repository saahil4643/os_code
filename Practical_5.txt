NETWORKING FUNDAMENTALS – PRACTICAL 5

Aim

To design and study a hybrid topology by combining two or more different
network topologies in Cisco Packet Tracer.

Requirement

-   Computer or laptop with Cisco Packet Tracer
-   Multiple PCs and at least two switches
-   One router if communication between different IP subnets is required
-   Copper straight-through cables

Theory

A hybrid network topology is formed by combining two or more basic
network topologies, such as star, bus, ring or tree. It is used when a
single topology does not meet the complete requirements of a network.
For example, separate groups of computers may be arranged in star
segments and those segments may be interconnected through switches or
routers. A hybrid design can provide flexibility and can be adapted to
the size and structure of an organization. Devices that communicate
within the same subnet need unique IP addresses in that subnet;
communication between different subnets requires routing.

Procedure

1.  Open Cisco Packet Tracer and place multiple PCs and switches in the
    workspace.
2.  Arrange the first group of PCs around a switch to form a star
    topology.
3.  Arrange a second group using another topology or switch segment.
4.  Connect the two segments through a central switch or router,
    according to the chosen design.
5.  Connect the devices using appropriate Ethernet cables.
6.  Assign unique IP addresses to PCs in the same subnet, or configure
    separate subnets and router interfaces if inter-network
    communication is required.
7.  Check that all physical links are active.
8.  Perform ping tests between devices that should be able to
    communicate.

Result

A hybrid network was designed in Packet Tracer, and connectivity between
the configured devices was tested.

Conclusion

The practical demonstrated how two or more network topologies can be
combined into a hybrid design. The resulting structure can be selected
according to network layout and communication requirements.
